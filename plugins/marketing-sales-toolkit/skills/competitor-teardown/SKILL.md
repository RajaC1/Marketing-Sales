# Competitor Teardown

## Inputs to gather
- Competitor name(s) and material to analyze (pasted copy, screenshots, or URLs the user provides)
- The user's own product/positioning for comparison
- What decision this teardown will inform (pricing, messaging, feature roadmap, sales battlecard)

## Analysis framework
1. **Positioning**: what core promise/category does the competitor claim? Who do they say it's for?
2. **Messaging pillars**: the 3–5 recurring themes in their copy (e.g., speed, compliance, ease of use).
3. **Pricing & packaging**: tiers, what's gated behind higher tiers, any anchoring tactics.
4. **Proof strategy**: what kind of social proof do they lean on (logos, numbers, case studies, reviews)?
5. **Weaknesses/gaps**: claims that are vague, missing proof, or objections they don't address.
6. **Opportunity map**: where the user's product can credibly claim an advantage — only based on what the user has told you about their own product, not assumptions.

## Rules
- Only analyze content actually provided or fetched — do not fabricate competitor pricing, features, or quotes from memory.
- If asked to browse a live competitor site and no fetch tool is available, say so and ask the user to paste the content instead.
- Output ends with a one-page battlecard: "When they say X, we say Y" — 3 to 5 rows.
